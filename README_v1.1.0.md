## What Version 1.1.0 adds

This version advances the previous hybrid-conflict and BAcc-NOVELTY line of work by placing the civilian experience of severe pressure under fragmentation at the centre of analysis. It focuses on the breakdown of communication bridges between civilians and decision-making centres; uncertainty and contradictory institutional signals; the distribution or opacity of decision authority; and the possibility that prolonged non-response can leave civilian populations exposed to escalating harm.

The new central author-supplied reflection is preserved verbatim in Persian above and translated separately into English. The English version is explanatory and does not replace the Persian source.

## Why the issue matters

Atrocity prevention cannot rely solely on retrospective documentation or late-stage humanitarian response. The UN Framework of Analysis for Atrocity Crimes identifies risk factors and indicators to guide structured monitoring; it explicitly treats the framework as a preventive tool, not a mechanical prediction instrument. This project proposes an additional systems lens: examine whether civilians can communicate safely with accountable institutions, whether those institutions can coordinate across fragmented authority, and whether warning signals lead to timely protective action.

The project's urgency is especially pronounced over short and medium horizons where rapid technological change, AI-enabled decision support, digital infrastructure dependence, information disorder, and institutional delay may interact. This is a reason for precautionary governance and readiness—not a claim that technology alone causes atrocities.

## Core terms

- **Civilian compression (proposed construct):** cumulative pressure, constraint, uncertainty, and loss of effective access to protection or accountable decision channels experienced by civilians amid conflict-related fragmentation.
- **Communication bridges:** trusted and accessible channels connecting civilians, local intermediaries, humanitarian organizations, public services, and responsible decision-makers.
- **Decision-centre topology:** the configuration of centralized, semi-centralized, decentralized, overlapping, or shifting authority and accountability.
- **Fragmentation:** breakdown, multiplication, or misalignment of institutions, social networks, information channels, authority, and service coordination.
- **Hybrid designation:** the processes through which people, events, information, and infrastructures are classified or attributed in contested hybrid environments; studied here for bias, uncertainty, consequences, and safeguards—not operational targeting.
- **BAcc-NOVELTY:** Balanced Acceleration of Novelty in Harmony with Hightech Revolution; a proposed principle for balancing innovation speed with evidence, rights, accountability, institutional capacity, and reversibility.

## Research architecture

`Observe public-interest conditions → map civilian communication and service pathways → assess fragmentation and uncertainty → apply atrocity-risk safeguards → deliberate with affected communities → choose proportionate preventive action → monitor outcomes → revise assumptions.`

The architecture includes feedback and feed-forward loops. It is not a linear command chain and does not require disclosure of sensitive personal or operational information.

## Research status and limits

This is a conceptual, interdisciplinary, hypothesis-generating, pre-empirical framework. It has not been empirically validated, and no predictive performance or causal effect is claimed. The terms “civilian compression” and “communication bridges” are proposed constructs in this version. Existing concepts such as civilian harm, human security, atrocity-risk factors, early warning, social fragmentation, and institutional trust have substantial prior literature. The project’s candidate contribution is their explicit integration into a civilian-centred, communication-and-decision-topology model linked to institutional response and BAcc-NOVELTY. Novelty remains a proposition for systematic review and testing.
