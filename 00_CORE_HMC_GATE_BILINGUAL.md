## Central research proposition

This version proposes that peace-oriented institutions need a continuously updated, civilian-centred understanding of how hybrid conflict systems distribute decision authority, intelligence production, influence, and coercive capacity across state, non-state, commercial, technical, and transnational actors. The aim is not to reproduce military intelligence functions. It is to improve public-interest situational awareness, civilian-risk anticipation, institutional accountability, and the capacity to prevent escalation and protect the conditions of Positive Peace.

The four author-supplied labels are retained as **provisional constructs**:

1. **Dynamical Hybrid Designations** — provisional label for the changing attribution, classification, role-assignment, and designation processes through which actors, entities, events, or infrastructures are framed within hybrid-conflict environments. The author should confirm whether “designations” is intended to refer primarily to actor categorization, target designation, institutional mandates, or a broader classificatory process. This repository does not operationalize target selection.
2. **Dynamical Hybrid Intelligence Gathering** — provisional label for the evolving, multi-source collection, interpretation, validation, and circulation of information in hybrid-conflict environments, examined from the standpoint of civilian protection, public accountability, and epistemic reliability.
3. **Dynamical Hybrid Hegemony** — provisional label for shifting asymmetries of agenda-setting, dependency, narrative influence, technological control, information access, and institutional leverage across hybrid networks.
4. **Dynamical Hybrid Wars** — provisional umbrella label for interacting overt/covert, kinetic/non-kinetic, military/non-military forms of coercion and competition. It is an analytical phrase, not a separate category of armed conflict under international law.

## Proposed core question

How can peace-oriented institutions identify and assess changing centres and networks of decision authority in hybrid-conflict environments—without reproducing surveillance, militarization, or partisan intelligence practices—in order to reduce civilian harm, strengthen institutional accountability, and improve the adaptive capacity of sustainable peace and Positive Peace systems?

## Foundational safeguards

- The term “hybrid” does not suspend international law or create a legal grey zone. Whether an armed conflict exists is determined by applicable legal criteria and facts, not by political labels.
- Civilian protection, human rights, privacy, due process, humanitarian access, and the protection of essential civilian infrastructure are non-negotiable constraints.
- The project is a peace-research and governance framework. It does not provide targeting methods, operational intelligence collection instructions, vulnerability exploitation, or tactical guidance.
- The concepts are not empirically validated. They require precise definitions, systematic prior-art review, operational measures, independent scrutiny, and pilot evaluation.
