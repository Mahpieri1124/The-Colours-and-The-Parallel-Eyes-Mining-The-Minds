# Research Map and Methodological Architecture

## 1. Research purpose

To develop and test a civilian-centred, multi-level framework for understanding how hybrid-conflict decision systems evolve and how peace-oriented institutions can recognize, assess, and respond to their consequences in ways that support sustainable peace and Positive Peace.

## 2. Main research question

How do centralized, semi-centralized, decentralized, and dynamically reconfiguring decision architectures shape the production and use of intelligence, the distribution of influence and responsibility, and the direct or indirect consequences of hybrid conflict for civilian populations—and how can peace institutions adapt without compromising rights, impartiality, legitimacy, or humanitarian principles?

## 3. Sub-questions

1. Which institutional and technical arrangements concentrate, distribute, or reconfigure consequential decision authority?
2. How do information provenance, uncertainty, data quality, and AI-mediated analysis affect institutional interpretation?
3. How do technological dependencies and information asymmetries shape hegemony and policy choices?
4. Through which pathways do hybrid-conflict dynamics affect civilian services, livelihoods, safety, trust, and social cohesion?
5. What institutional capacities are required for early recognition, lawful response, independent scrutiny, and correction?
6. Can HMC-GATE and BAcc-NOVELTY improve the quality and timing of peace-oriented decisions?
7. Which indicators can assess contributions to Positive Peace without overstating causality?

## 4. Construct map

| Construct | Working meaning | Unit of analysis | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dynamical Hybrid Designations | Changing classification, attribution, and role-designation processes | Institutional labels, event assessments, accountability records | Provisional; author clarification required |
| Dynamical Hybrid Intelligence Gathering | Information collection, validation, interpretation, and sharing ecosystem | Information-governance processes | Provisional |
| Dynamical Hybrid Hegemony | Dynamic asymmetries of agenda-setting, dependency, influence, and control | Relationships among institutions, platforms, states, and communities | Provisional |
| Dynamical Hybrid Wars | Interacting military/non-military and overt/covert coercive competition | Conflict environment and civilian-impact pathways | Provisional analytical umbrella, not legal category |
| HMC-GATE | Human metacognitive review of assumptions, evidence, blind spots, responsibility, and consequences | Decision and review process | Author's framework; operational protocol to be tested |
| BAcc-NOVELTY | Balanced Acceleration of Novelty in Harmony with Hightech Revolution | Institutional innovation and response choices | Proposed decision principle |
| Positive Peace | Conditions and institutions associated with peaceful societies | Societal and institutional context | Established research field; specify selected framework |
| Governance Recognition Lag | Time between emergence of a material change and its recognition by a relevant institution | Event–institution pair | Candidate measure; requires precise operational definition |

## 5. Research work packages

### WP1 — Concept clarification and prior-art review (Months 0–6)
- Confirm author-intended meanings of all four Dynamical Hybrid constructs.
- Build a transparent search protocol across Scopus, Web of Science, Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore, ACM Digital Library, ICRC, SIPRI, UN, OECD, and relevant regional research repositories.
- Publish search strings, dates, inclusion/exclusion criteria, screening flow, and a construct-overlap matrix.
- Distinguish established terms, authorial labels, metaphors, and candidate technical constructs.

**Deliverable:** concept dictionary and systematic/scoping review protocol.

### WP2 — Dynamic institutional and decision-architecture mapping (Months 4–10)
- Identify publicly documented institutional roles and mandates.
- Distinguish formal authority from practical influence, technical control, information access, and legal responsibility.
- Use case-level, non-operational mapping; exclude sensitive personal data and exploitable operational details.
- Track changes over time rather than relying on static organization charts.

**Deliverable:** typology and public-interest mapping template.

### WP3 — Information integrity and epistemic governance (Months 6–12)
- Define provenance, corroboration, uncertainty, correction, and contestability standards.
- Examine AI decision-support as a source of both analytic capacity and potential error.
- Assess privacy, data minimization, source protection, bias, automation bias, and adversarial manipulation.

**Deliverable:** information-quality and accountability framework.

### WP4 — Civilian-impact pathways (Months 8–16)
Assess direct, indirect, cumulative, cross-border, and delayed consequences for:
- health and medical services;
- water, energy, transport, communications, and finance;
- food security and livelihoods;
- education and mobility;
- humanitarian access;
- privacy, safety, and protection;
- public trust, social cohesion, and local agency.

**Deliverable:** civilian-impact pathway model and indicator catalogue.

### WP5 — HMC-GATE and BAcc-NOVELTY protocol (Months 12–20)
- Define review questions, decision records, escalation triggers, and independent challenge procedures.
- Compare “proceed,” “bounded pilot,” “gather evidence,” “redesign,” “pause,” and “stop” options.
- Assess both the cost of premature action and the cost of unjustified delay.
- Require clear human accountability and avenues for affected communities to contest decisions.

**Deliverable:** HMC-GATE/BAcc-NOVELTY institutional review protocol.

### WP6 — Peace-institution design and pilot evaluation (Months 18–30)
- Co-design with local peacebuilders, humanitarian organizations, civil society, relevant public institutions, and independent researchers.
- Pilot in a carefully selected, non-sensitive civilian-protection or peacebuilding context.
- Use process evaluation and comparison designs where feasible.
- Pre-register measurable outcomes where appropriate.
- Conduct independent ethical and legal review.

**Deliverable:** pilot report, limitations, and revision proposal.

### WP7 — Replication and scale assessment (Months 30+)
- Replicate across different institutional and regional contexts.
- Test whether the framework adds value beyond existing early-warning, conflict analysis, civilian-protection, and anticipatory-governance methods.
- Retain, revise, merge, or retire constructs according to evidence.

**Deliverable:** replication assessment and Version 2 roadmap.

## 6. Candidate indicators

| Dimension | Candidate indicator | Important caution |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition | Governance Recognition Lag | Define the event and recognition threshold in advance. |
| Decision quality | Proportion of material claims with provenance and uncertainty recorded | More documentation does not automatically mean better judgement. |
| Accountability | Share of consequential decisions with a named accountable authority and review route | Formal attribution may differ from actual influence. |
| Contestability | Availability and use of safe correction/appeal channels | Measure safety and accessibility, not just existence. |
| Civilian impact | Service disruption duration, affected population estimates, and recovery time | Disaggregate carefully; avoid exposing individuals. |
| Humanitarian access | Access constraints and time to restore access | Context and security conditions matter. |
| Institutional learning | Time from verified adverse finding to corrective action | Do not reward superficial or rushed corrections. |
| BAcc-NOVELTY | Balance between avoidable delay and premature/unsafe deployment | Requires context-specific thresholds and counterfactual reasoning. |
| Positive Peace | Selected institutional and societal indicators | Do not infer peace from one metric or claim causality without design support. |

## 7. Methodological design

A mixed-methods programme is recommended:
- scoping/systematic literature review;
- comparative institutional case studies;
- public-document and policy analysis;
- structured expert interviews with consent and risk review;
- participatory civilian-impact assessment;
- process tracing for carefully bounded cases;
- network and systems mapping using public, non-sensitive information;
- pilot evaluation and independent replication.

No single method should be treated as sufficient. Quantitative indicators require construct validity, reliable data, and transparent uncertainty. Qualitative evidence should include dissenting accounts and reflexive consideration of researcher positionality.

## 8. Ethics, law, and data governance

- Do not collect sensitive personal data unless necessary, lawful, proportionate, and approved under a robust ethics protocol.
- Do not publish identities or network maps that could expose civilians, local peacebuilders, sources, or humanitarian personnel to retaliation.
- Do not infer hostile intent from political dissent, ethnicity, religion, nationality, or lawful association.
- Do not present allegations as verified facts.
- Apply data minimization, access control, secure retention, and deletion schedules.
- Obtain informed consent for interviews and participatory research.
- Ensure independent review and accessible correction mechanisms.
- Treat international humanitarian law and human rights law as applicable according to their respective scopes; “hybrid” terminology does not displace legal obligations.

## 9. Decision logic under BAcc-NOVELTY

For each proposed institutional innovation or intervention:
1. Define the public-interest problem and affected groups.
2. Record evidence, uncertainty, alternative explanations, and missing perspectives.
3. Assess expected benefit, severity of harm, distributional effects, and reversibility.
4. Confirm legality, rights safeguards, accountability, and contestability.
5. Assess institutional and community readiness.
6. Select proportionate action: proceed, bounded pilot, gather evidence, redesign, pause, or stop.
7. Monitor outcomes and unintended effects.
8. Revise the decision when evidence changes.

**Companion rule:** No unjustified or avoidable delay — but also no unjustified continuation or acceleration.

## 10. Indicative timeline

0–6 months: concept clarification and literature review.  
6–12 months: institutional mapping and information-governance design.  
12–20 months: civilian-impact model and HMC-GATE/BAcc-NOVELTY protocol.
